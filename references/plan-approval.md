# Getting the plan approved

This covers how to package and sell the plan. These are common practices from experienced B2B marketing leaders, in my own words.

## Start with finance

Before building anything, meet with the CFO or finance partner. Learn:
- The ratios they watch (marketing spend as a share of revenue, CAC, payback)
- What "good" looks like to them
- The budget range they're expecting

Show up to planning knowing the guardrails. Pitch marketing as an investment, not a cost.

## Two kinds of metrics

- Executive metrics: pipeline, bookings, revenue, CAC, ROI. These go in the plan and the board deck.
- Operating metrics: conversion rates, traffic, engagement. These run the team. They go in the appendix.

Don't lead with impressions or clicks unless you can tie them to money.

## Tie marketing to company goals

Align early with sales, product, and finance. If the company goals aren't clear yet, propose marketing's piece yourself. Keep the plan to a few themes for the year instead of a long list of tactics. Tactics change in March. Themes hold up.

## Top-down, bottom-up, or hybrid

- Top-down: leadership hands each function a budget.
- Bottom-up: teams submit detailed asks.
- Hybrid: leadership sets ranges, teams plan inside them. This is usually the best call.

## Three budget buckets

1. Strategic programs tied straight to revenue goals (roughly 55 to 75%)
2. Fixed costs that aren't strategic (tools, legal, basics)
3. Experiments (roughly 10 to 20%)

Show how each bucket maps to a company goal.

## Plan for tradeoffs

Flag risky or dependent bets early. Show what changes if pipeline efficiency drops. For each experiment, show the upside and the fallback.

## Talk like an exec

Say "this adds $2M in pipeline" instead of "MQLs up 30%." Be honest about what you don't know. For the board, keep it short, lead with revenue, and move backup to the appendix.

## One page

The final plan should fit on one page. Everything else is appendix. Then turn the approved plan into a team kickoff so it actually gets run.
