# Kellogg on planning

This is the spine of the skill. It's a summary in my own words of ideas from Dave Kellogg's Kellblog. Read the original posts linked in `sources.md`. They're better than any summary.

## 1. Plan vs. budget

A plan is a set of strategic choices. A budget is what those choices cost. You can tell which one you built by how you'd cut it.

- If the CFO says "cut 15%" and your answer is to trim every line, you built a budget.
- If your answer is "we'll pause the EMEA launch and keep everything else," you built a plan.

Use this test in Phase 3. Make the user rank their objectives so the cut is already decided before anyone asks.

## 2. Look back before you look forward

A good operating plan shows three things side by side:
1. Last year's history (2026 actuals or best forecast)
2. This year's forecast (where 2026 will land)
3. The proposed plan (2027), with a multi-year line attached

The board needs to see the trajectory, not just one year. That's why the model includes a 2028 out-year built from the same drivers as 2027, not a made-up number.

## 3. The ARR leaky bucket

Show ARR as a bucket:
- Starting ARR
- Plus new logo ARR
- Plus expansion ARR
- Minus churn (customers who leave) and downsell (customers who shrink)
- Equals ending ARR

Pair it with burn. Burn ratio (cash burned divided by net new ARR) tells the board how much it costs to add a dollar of ARR.

Also show a SaaS P&L summary: gross margin, CAC ratio, Rule of 40, and ending cash.

## 4. Driver-based planning

Build the smallest set of drivers that gets you an accurate-enough P&L. In SaaS that's usually rep productivity, bookings mix, renewal rates, and margin percentages. Add a driver only if it changes the answer. Then prune the ones that don't move anything.

This keeps the model simple enough that a CEO can follow it and change an assumption live in a meeting.

## 5. Three scenarios

Kellogg compares three cases:
- Baseline
- Growth (spend more, grow faster)
- Profit (spend less, burn less)

Show each with its out-year model so the board can see where each path leads in 2028, not just 2027.

## 6. A plan you can beat

The base plan should be one the team expects to beat. Missing plan erodes trust with the board faster than a slightly lower target does. Put stretch goals in the Growth case or in comp plans, not in the board plan.

## 7. Board questions on the marketing budget

See `board-questions.md` for the full list used in the red-team phase. The big themes:
- Where's the money going (people, programs, infrastructure) and how has the mix changed?
- Is there marketing money hiding outside marketing?
- Are the CMO and CRO aligned?
- Does the demand gen plan show pipe/spend and cost per oppty, and do those ratios match history? If they improve, why?
- How much is going to hard-to-measure work like brand, and can the CMO defend it?
- Will this CMO deliver if we fund this?

## 8. You can't eat pipegen

Pipeline generation is a means. Starting each quarter with enough coverage is the goal. A marketing team can hit its pipegen number and the company can still miss if the pipeline lands in the wrong quarters. See `capacity-and-pipeline.md`.
