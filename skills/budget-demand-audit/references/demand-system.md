# The demand system

How this skill thinks about demand, in my own words.

## One revenue plan, not two

Most companies plan in a chain. Marketing hits an MQL number, sales converts it, and each team plans in its own silo. A better way is to build one plan for pipeline and revenue using everything the revenue team has, then split it into team plans after.

This lines up with Kellogg's four sources of pipeline. The skill builds one pipeline plan across all sources first.

## Capture first, then create

1. Capture. Map where pipeline comes from today. Usually 3 to 6 sources: website and inbound, events, outbound, partners, paid lead gen. Compare each source by what it produces and how well it closes (use win rate to compare quality, not just volume). Decide what to scale, fix, or cut.
2. Create. Capture is capped by how many buyers are in market right now. To grow past that, you have to create demand with buyers who aren't shopping yet: education, content, community, and brand. Budget this separately from capture.

## The waste audit

Low-intent leads look cheap but cost a lot. They eat SDR time, inflate headcount, and drag down win rates. Split leads into:
- Declared intent: they asked to talk to sales
- Low intent: they downloaded something and we assumed interest

Measure how much budget goes to low-intent sources and what share of pipeline they really produce. Often a real slice of budget can be cut with little pipeline impact.

## Measurement

Software attribution gives credit to the last trackable click (search, direct). Buyers often say a podcast, a peer, or a community sent them. Add self-reported attribution ("How did you hear about us?") on high-intent forms as a second lens. Use it next to pipe/spend, not instead of it. See `tensions.md`.

## When the goal is the problem

If the model can't reach the target without scaling waste, the target may be the problem. Use CAC and cost per pipeline dollar to reset the conversation. Then put money back into demand creation slowly and track it.
