# AI-era assumptions (Phase 9)

This phase exists because 2027 plans will be full of AI promises. The job here is to keep the real gains and catch the fake ones. Apply Kellogg's test to every one: what's the mechanism, and how will we know by mid-year?

## The five questions

Walk the user through each. Write down the answer, the number it changes in the model, and the proof point.

### 1. Headcount leverage
Which roles will you hire fewer of because of AI? Common candidates: SDRs, content writers, marketing ops, campaign managers, junior designers.
- What work does AI take over, exactly?
- How many hours per week does that free up today, measured, not guessed?
- Does the freed time turn into more output or fewer hires? Pick one. You can't count it twice.
- What new roles do you need instead (AI ops, GTM engineer, data owner)?

### 2. Agents in pipeline work
Are AI agents handling inbound response, research, first-touch outbound, or meeting booking?
- What share of 2026 pipeline did agents touch, and how did it convert vs. human-sourced pipeline?
- Agent-sourced pipeline gets its own source line and its own conversion rates in the funnel. Don't blend it in until you have a few quarters of data.

### 3. Answer engines and AEO
Buyers increasingly research through AI assistants and answer engines. That shifts value toward first-party content, trust, and being cited.
- What share of 2026 inbound mentions an AI assistant in self-reported attribution?
- What's the plan and budget for being cited (first-party research, docs, reviews, community presence)?
- How will you measure it? (Self-reported attribution, branded search, AI visibility tools if you trust them.)
- Expect some loss of classic organic search traffic. Don't plan on 2024 SEO numbers.

### 4. AI tools as a real budget line
AI tools, model usage, data enrichment, and agent platforms belong in the infrastructure budget. Many teams leave them scattered across cards and other budgets. Pull them in so the board sees the true cost, and compare that cost to the headcount it replaces.

### 5. Data readiness
AI only works on clean data. Ask:
- Is CRM stage data trustworthy enough to compute close rates?
- Who owns data quality?
- What has to be fixed in Q1 before the AI gains in this plan can happen?

## Rules for the model

- A gain with a mechanism and a Q2 proof point can go in Baseline.
- A gain without a mechanism goes in Growth (upside) only.
- Every AI gain gets a line in the assumptions log with an owner and a check-in date.
- Show the board a one-line "AI in this plan" summary: what we're counting on, what it saves, and how we'll know.
