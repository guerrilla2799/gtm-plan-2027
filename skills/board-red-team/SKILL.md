# Board red-team

Part of the 2027 Marketing Planning Skill Pack. Use it on any plan, even one built outside the pack. For building a full plan from scratch, use the `gtm-plan-2027` skill.

The marketing budget questions are paraphrased from Dave Kellogg's Kellblog post on board-level questions about the marketing budget. See `references/sources.md`. He isn't affiliated with this skill.

## Ground rules

1. Be the tough board member, not the cheerleader. The goal is to find the weak spots before the board does.
2. Quote the plan when scoring. If you can't point to where the plan answers a question, it's Missing.
3. Don't invent the company's numbers. If a number is needed and absent, say so.
4. Any ratio that improves vs. last year needs a named reason. If there isn't one, flag it as "spreadsheet magic."

## Workflow

### 1. Get the plan
Ask for the plan, deck, budget, or model. Files, pasted text, or a link they can export all work. Ask two short questions:
- Who's the audience (board, CEO, CFO)?
- When is the meeting?

### 2. Score every question
Read `references/board-questions.md`. For each of the 21 questions:
- Score: Strong, Weak, or Missing
- Evidence: where in the plan it's answered (short quote or slide number)
- Fix: one sentence on what would make it Strong

Use `references/kellogg-planning.md` for what good looks like on plan vs. budget, the ARR bridge, scenarios, and the marketing budget. Use `references/ai-era-assumptions.md` for the AI questions. Use `references/tensions.md` where the plan picks a side on attribution, brand spend, or efficiency.

### 3. Find the top 5 fixes
Rank the Weak and Missing items by how likely the board is to ask and how much damage a bad answer does. Write the five fixes in order, each with the exact change to make to the plan or deck.

### 4. Prep the answers
For the 8 questions the board is most likely to ask, draft a two- to three-sentence answer in the presenter's voice, using only facts from the plan. Where the plan doesn't support a good answer, say what they need to add.

### 5. Deliver
1. Scorecard table: question, score, evidence, fix
2. Top 5 fixes
3. Likely questions with suggested answers
4. A one-line overall read: ready, close, or not ready, and why

## Style
Direct and specific. No flattery. Plain words. Lead with the problem, then the fix.
