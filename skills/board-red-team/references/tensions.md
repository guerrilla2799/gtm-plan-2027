# Where the ideas pull in different directions

Kellogg's math wins. Other voices fill gaps. Name these tensions in the plan when they come up. Boards respect a CMO who sees the tradeoff.

| Topic | The tension | How this skill settles it |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of the funnel | Many demand leaders push back on MQLs as a planning unit. Many teams still plan around leads. | Plan on opportunities and pipeline dollars (Kellogg's unit). Track leads only as an operating metric. |
| Attribution | Software attribution undervalues demand creation. Kellogg wants pipe/spend and cost per oppty by channel. | Keep Kellogg's ratios as the primary scorecard. Add self-reported attribution as a second lens. If the two disagree, show both and explain the gap. |
| Brand and demand creation spend | Demand creation needs a real budget. Kellogg's board asks whether "squishy" spend like brand can be defended. | Fund demand creation as its own line with a clear goal and leading indicators (branded search, direct traffic, self-reported mentions, win rate on those deals). Size it so the plan still works if it's slow to pay off. |
| Efficiency gains | Optimists bake in better ratios. Kellogg asks if it's spreadsheet magic. | Any ratio better than 2026 needs a named reason and a mid-year test. Otherwise it moves to the upside scenario. |
| Plan detail | Approval wants a few themes on one page. Kellogg's model is detailed. | Both. One-page plan up front, full model in the appendix. |
| Experiments budget | A 10 to 20% experiments bucket is common. Kellogg wants every dollar tied to pipeline or an objective. | Keep an experiments bucket, but give each test a hypothesis, a kill date, and the metric that would make it scale. |
| AI efficiency | Many vendors promise big AI headcount savings. Kellogg would ask for proof. | See `ai-era-assumptions.md`. Gains need a mechanism and a test, or they go in upside. |
